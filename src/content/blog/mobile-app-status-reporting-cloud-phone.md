Running mobile app tasks is only half the work. The other half is explaining what happened.

Managers do not want a pile of screenshots. Operators do not want to open every cloud phone again. Clients do not want vague updates. A good status report turns cloud phone task results into something people can act on.

## Quick answer

A mobile app status report should summarize task coverage, ready accounts, failed accounts, failure categories, AI recovery actions, human review items, blocked workflows, and next steps. Cloud phone task logs make this possible when they record device, account group, script version, final status, and evidence.

## Why status reports matter

Mobile workflows often involve many accounts and small steps. Without a report, the team may know that “some tasks ran” but not what that means.

A useful report answers:

- how many cloud phones were checked;
- which accounts are ready;
- which accounts failed;
- why they failed;
- what AI recovered;
- what needs human review;
- what should happen next.

That is the difference between activity and operations.

## What to include

Use a simple structure:

- project or campaign;
- date and time;
- cloud phone group;
- task name;
- script version;
- total devices checked;
- ready count;
- failed count;
- failure categories;
- AI recovered count;
- human review count;
- blocked items;
- next owner.

This format works for internal teams and client-facing summaries.

## Keep failure categories readable

Do not report only raw errors.

Use categories that humans understand:

- login expired;
- permission missing;
- expected page not found;
- app update suspected;
- account warning;
- unknown popup;
- timeout;
- script needs update.

These categories help people decide what to do.

## Where AI helps

AI can summarize task logs into a clear report, group similar failures, and highlight unusual patterns.

For example, if most failures come from one app version, that belongs in the report. If AI recovered ten known popups, that should also be visible. If three accounts need human review, they should not be buried in a long log.

AI should summarize evidence, not replace it. The report should link back to logs or screenshots when needed.

## FAQ

### Should every task generate a report?

Important batch tasks should. Small tests may only need logs.

### Who should read the report?

Operators, managers, account owners, support leads, or clients depending on the workflow.

### What makes a report useful?

Clear next actions. A report that does not help someone decide what to do next is too vague.
