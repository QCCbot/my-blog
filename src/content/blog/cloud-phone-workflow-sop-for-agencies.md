Agencies often manage mobile work for more than one client, market, or campaign.

That creates a coordination problem. One person knows the client. Another person knows the script. Someone else checks task results. If the process lives only in chat messages, mistakes become likely.

## Quick answer

Agencies using cloud phones should create a simple SOP for each recurring mobile workflow: device group, account owner, script version, approval steps, safe AI recovery rules, human review cases, reporting format, and handoff notes. The SOP keeps batch mobile work consistent across clients.

## Why agencies need SOPs

Agency work has more context switching than in-house operations.

One team may handle:

- multiple client accounts;
- different app regions;
- different content calendars;
- separate approval rules;
- different risk tolerance;
- different reporting needs.

Without an SOP, operators rely on memory. That works until someone is absent, a task is urgent, or an app changes.

## What the SOP should include

Keep it practical.

For each workflow, write down:

- client or project name;
- cloud phone group;
- target app;
- account owner;
- script name and version;
- task schedule;
- expected start screen;
- expected success screen;
- safe recovery cases;
- manual review cases;
- reporting owner;
- escalation contact.

This gives every operator the same map.

## Where AI fits in the SOP

AI should be described in the SOP, not left vague.

Include:

- whether AI script generation is allowed;
- who reviews AI-generated script changes;
- which exceptions AI may recover;
- where AI must stop;
- what logs must be captured after AI takeover.

This makes AI a controlled assistant instead of an invisible decision-maker.

## A useful handoff note

Every shift or campaign handoff should include:

- what ran;
- what succeeded;
- what failed;
- what was retried;
- what AI recovered;
- what needs human review;
- what should not be touched yet.

This is especially important when tasks run across many accounts.

## FAQ

### Does every client need a separate workflow?

Usually yes. Even if scripts are similar, approval rules and account risks may differ.

### Should agencies let AI recover failures automatically?

Only for approved low-risk cases. Client-sensitive actions should remain visible and reviewable.

### What is the first SOP to create?

Start with the workflow that repeats most often and causes the most handoff confusion.
